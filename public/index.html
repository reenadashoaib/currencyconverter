Reading the rate

What the mid-market rate is

It’s the midpoint between the price banks pay to buy a currency and the price they sell it for. It’s the fairest reference point, and it’s the rate this converter uses.

Why your bank quotes a different number

Banks, card providers and exchange counters add a margin on top of the mid-market rate, and some add a fee as well. Compare their quote with the result here to see how much you’re paying.

How often the rates change

Currency markets move all day on weekdays. The rates here are refreshed once a day, which is right for budgeting, invoices and travel planning but not for trading.

Sharing a conversion

The page address updates as you convert, so you can bookmark it or send it to someone. Use “Copy result” to paste the figure into a message or spreadsheet.

Questions

Is the converter free?

Yes. There’s no sign-up and no limit on how many conversions you make. The site is paid for by the ads you see on the page.

Where do the exchange rates come from?

Rates come from ExchangeRate-API, which publishes daily reference rates for about 160 currencies. If it can’t be reached, the converter falls back to European Central Bank rates for around 30 major currencies.

Can I use these rates to pay or get paid?

Use them as a guide. The amount you actually pay or receive depends on the rate and fees your bank or payment service charges at the moment the money moves.

Does it work offline?

The last rates you loaded are saved in your browser. If your connection drops, the converter uses those and tells you when they were last updated.

Which currencies are listed first?

The currencies people convert most often appear under “Popular” at the top of each list. Every other currency follows in alphabetical order by name.

Rates are mid-market reference rates for information only. They aren't an offer to buy or sell currency, and your bank or card provider will use its own rate.
